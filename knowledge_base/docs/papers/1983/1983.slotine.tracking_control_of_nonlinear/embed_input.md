Tracking Control of Nonlinear Systems Using Sliding Surfaces

Topics include Sliding mode control, Nonlinear control, Robust control, Trajectory tracking, Chattering, Robot manipulators, Sampled-data control.

<!-- chunk {"id": "summary-0002", "role": "summary", "section": "Summary", "weight": 2.0} -->

Develops sliding-surface feedback control for robust tracking in nonlinear time-varying systems with disturbances and parameter uncertainty. Continuous approximations reduce high-frequency control activity while trading tracking precision against robustness to unmodelled dynamics; the thesis also addresses measurement uncertainty, sampling, and a two-link manipulator carrying variable loads.

<!-- chunk {"id": "abstract-0003", "role": "abstract", "section": "Abstract", "weight": 2.0} -->

A feedback control methodology is developed to achieve accurate tracking in a class of nonlinear, time-varying systems in the presence of disturbances and parameter variations. The methodology uses in its idealized form piecewise continuous feedback control, resulting in the state trajectory 'sliding' along a time-varying sliding surface in the state space. This idealized control law in theory achieves perfect tracking, but is accompanied by an undesirable high frequency component in the control variable. To rectify this situation, continuous control laws are used to approximate the discontinuous control laws and obtain robust tracking: a trade-off is made between tracking accuracy and robustness to unmodelled dynamics. The implementation of the control methodology in the presence of bounded measurement uncertainty and of data sampling is discussed. The control of a two-link manipulator handling variable loads in a flexible manufacturing environment illustrates the methodology.
