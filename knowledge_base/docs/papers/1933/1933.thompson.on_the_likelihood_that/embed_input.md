On the Likelihood That One Unknown Probability Exceeds Another in View of the Evidence of Two Samples

Topics include Thompson sampling, Probability matching, Bayesian decision theory, Multi-armed bandits, Sequential experiments, Clinical trials, Adaptive allocation.

Thompson proposes allocating future trials in proportion to the posterior probability that one treatment is better than another, rather than waiting for a large fixed sample before acting. In modern terms, the paper introduces probability matching for sequential decision-making and is the earliest source for what is now called Thompson sampling in bandit problems.

In elaborating the relations of the present communication interest was not centred upon the interpretation of particular data, but grew out of a general interest in problems of research planning. From this point of view there can be no objection to the use of data, however meagre, as a guide to action required before more can be collected; although serious objection can otherwise be raised to argument based upon a small number of observations. Indeed, the fact that such objection can never be eliminated entirely, no matter how great the number of observations, suggested the possible value of seeking other modes of operation than that of taking a large number of observations before analysis or any attempt to direct our course. This problem is more general than that treated in Section 2, and is directly concerned with any case where probable error may be established by means of which we judge whether one mode of operation is better than another in some given sense or not....
