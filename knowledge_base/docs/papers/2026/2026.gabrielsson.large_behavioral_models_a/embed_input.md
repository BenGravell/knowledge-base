Large Behavioral Models: A Foundation Model Paradigm for Human Actions

Topics include Large behavioral models, Next-event prediction, Human actions, Behavioral representations, Zero-shot learning, Enterprise artificial intelligence.

Defines Large Behavioral Models as foundation models that learn meaning from chronological sequences of human actions, transactions, and interaction logs rather than from static text or image co-occurrence alone. <!-- chunk {"id": "abstract-0003", "role": "abstract", "section": "Abstract", "weight": 2.0} -->

Foundation models have achieved remarkable success by learning from large-scale, unstructured data in domains such as language, vision, and genomics. Yet an often-overlooked source of massive, rich information is behavioral data: the discrete actions, transactions, and interaction logs generated by billions of users across sectors including retail, payments, app usage, and workforce management. In this paper, we introduce Large Behavioral Models (LBMs). By framing behavioral data as chronological sequences of discrete events and employing a next-event prediction objective, LBMs learn powerful behavioral representations of both agents (users) and items (products, services, tasks), along with the features that describe them. We define a new paradigm where user actions define meaning in a manner complementary and often orthogonal to the semantics captured by Large Language Models (LLMs) and Large Vision Models (LVMs). We instantiate this approach with BehaviorGPT, a frontier LBM that exhibits strong zero-shot capabilities. In real-world trials with leading enterprise partners, BehaviorGPT improves predictive accuracy and decision-making, including double-digit sales uplift for retailers and payment companies.
