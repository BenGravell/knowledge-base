Uniform Colour Spaces Based on Colour Appearance Model

Topics include Color appearance, Uniform color spaces Color difference, Colorimetry, Visual perception.

<!-- chunk {"id": "summary-0002", "role": "summary", "section": "Summary", "weight": 2.0} -->

Builds and tests uniform color spaces derived from the appearance model, comparing them against multiple color-discrimination datasets. The work helped consolidate -based color-difference spaces for applications where perceptual uniformity matters.

<!-- chunk {"id": "abstract-0003", "role": "abstract", "section": "Abstract", "weight": 2.0} -->

Can a single colour model be used for all colorimetric applications? This article intends to answer that question. Colour appearance models have been developed to predict colour appearance under different viewing conditions. They are also capable of evaluating colour differences because of their embedded uniform colour spaces. This article first tests the performance of the CIE 2002 colour appearance model in predicting three types of colour discrimination data sets: large and small-magnitude colour differences under daylight illuminants and small-magnitude colour differences under illuminant A. The results showed that gave reasonable performance compared with the best available formulae and uniform colour spaces. It was further extended to give accurate predictions to all types of colour discrimination data. The results were very encouraging in that the extensions performed second best among all the colour models tested and only slightly poorer than the models that were developed to fit a particular data set. One extension derived to fit all types of data can predict well for colour differences having a large range of difference magnitudes.
