Computational Methods for Sparse Solution of Linear Inverse Problems

Topics include Sparse approximation, Compressed sensing, Linear inverse problems, Greedy algorithms, Convex relaxation, Iterative thresholding, Basis pursuit, Survey paper.

<!-- chunk {"id": "summary-0002", "role": "summary", "section": "Summary", "weight": 2.0} -->

Surveys the main algorithmic families for sparse linear inverse problems, including greedy pursuit, convex optimization, and thresholding-style methods. Its usefulness is practical as much as theoretical: it compares computational tradeoffs, recovery guarantees, and the regimes where different sparse approximation techniques tend to work well.

<!-- chunk {"id": "abstract-0003", "role": "abstract", "section": "Abstract", "weight": 2.0} -->

The goal of the sparse approximation problem is to approximate a target signal using a linear combination of a few elementary signals drawn from a fixed collection. This paper surveys the major practical algorithms for sparse approximation. Specific attention is paid to computational issues, to the circumstances in which individual methods tend to perform well, and to the theoretical guarantees available. Many fundamental questions in electrical engineering, statistics, and applied mathematics can be posed as sparse approximation problems, making these algorithms versatile and relevant to a plethora of applications.
