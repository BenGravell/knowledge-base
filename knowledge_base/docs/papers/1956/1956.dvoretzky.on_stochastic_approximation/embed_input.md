On Stochastic Approximation

Topics include Stochastic approximation, Robbins-monro process, Kiefer-wolfowitz process, Almost sure convergence, Mean-square convergence, Recursive estimation, Stochastic processes.

<!-- chunk {"id": "summary-0002", "role": "summary", "section": "Summary", "weight": 2.0} -->

Dvoretzky develops a general convergence framework for stochastic approximation schemes, treating Robbins-Monro and Kiefer-Wolfowitz as special cases of noisy self-correcting iterative processes. The paper is important because it strengthens convergence guarantees, especially mean-square and almost-sure convergence, and clarifies the stochastic-process conditions behind recursive approximation methods.

<!-- chunk {"id": "abstract-0003", "role": "abstract", "section": "Abstract", "weight": 2.0} -->

Stochastic approximation is concerned with schemes converging to some sought value when, due to the stochastic nature of the problem, the observations involve errors. The interesting schemes are those which are self-correcting, that is, in which a mistake always tends to be wiped out in the limit, and in which the convergence to the desired value is of some specified nature, for example, it is mean-square convergence. The typical example of such a scheme is the original one of Robbins-Monro for approximating, under suitable conditions, the point where a regression function assumes a given value.
