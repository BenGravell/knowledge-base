## Introduction

The problem of expected risk minimization is ubiquitous in machine learning and statistics [Shalev-Shwartz2013,Vapnik1998]. It is based on the idea that the quality of a model can be assessed by measuring its expected error, quantified by some loss function. The expectation should be evaluated with respect to the data-generating distribution. However, in practice, only samples are available. So the expectation needs to be replaced with a data-driven proxy, which aggregates the data. The common solution is empirical risk minimization or the sample average approach (SAA), where one takes an average over the losses at the sampled data points.

Despite its advantages, the SAA often exhibits excessive sensitivity to the specific data realizations, particularly in high-dimensional settings leading to diminished generalization capabilities of the model. To address this, researchers have turned to Distributionally Robust Optimization (DRO), aiming to robustify against disparities between the empirical and true data-generating distributions.

In DRO, a worst-case expectation with respect to distributions in an ambiguity set centered on the empirical distribution serves as a proxy for the true expected risk. This set can be based on the Wasserstein distance [Esfahani2018b], $\phi$-divergences [Ben-Tal2013], hypothesis tests [Bertsimas2009b], and others. See [Rahimian2019,Lin2022] for recent surveys. However, DRO faces challenges when determining the ambiguity set's size. Current approaches rely on concentration inequalities [Delage2010] or asymptotic bounds [Ben-Tal2013], ensuring that true distribution is contained within the ambiguity set with high probability. Unfortunately, this often results in conservatism, caused by either loose constants in the concentration inequalities or the shape of the ambiguity set. As an alternative, bootstrapping or cross validation techniques are often employed (cf. [Esfahani2018b]). These can be computationally expensive and lack statistical guarantees for finite samples, similarly to the asymptotic bounds. Such guarantees are a requirement in safety-critical applications like control (e.g. constraint tightening in tube-based MPC [Lorenzen2017, Aolaritei2023]).

To address conservativeness issues, [Duchi2021b,Lam2019] focus on bounding the expectation directly as an alternative to creating a confidence bound for the entire distribution. This mimics the focus on the expectation in the statistical learning framework of [Vapnik1998]. However, their bounds are asymptotic and therefore also lack strong statistical guarantees. In this paper, we take the first steps towards a finite-sample version of their scheme. To achieve this, we draw inspiration from results in order statistics [David2003] and stochastic orders [Shaked2007] to motivate the use of permutation invariant ambiguity sets. Notably, the $\phi$-divergences used in [Duchi2021b,Lam2019,Van2021] produce a specific case. We coin the term ordered risk minimizationto emphasize our statistical motivations. We demonstrate how to calibrate the ambiguity set's size to upper bound the true expectation with high probability, even when the true distribution does not fall within the ambiguity set. This probability serves as an intuitive tuning parameter.

## Problem Statement

We consider expected risk minimization $$\minimize_{\theta \in \Theta} \quad \E[\ell(\theta, \xi)].$$ Here $\Theta \subseteq \Re^{n_\theta}$ and $\xi \colon \Omega \to \Xi \subseteq \Re^{n_\xi}$ is a random vector on a probability space $(\Omega, \F, \prob)$. As is common [Vapnik1998,Shalev-Shwartz2013], we then assume access to independent and identically distributed (iid) samples $\xi^{}, \dots, \xi^{(n-1)}$. Let $\ell_i(\theta) = \ell(\theta, \xi^{(i)})$ for $i=1, \dots, n-1$ and take $\ell_n(\theta)$ such that it upper bounds the cost almost surely. That is $\prob[\ell(\theta, \xi) \leq \ell_n(\theta)] = 1$. It is assumed that $\ell_n(\theta)$ is finite for any $\theta$. We do so for two reasons: (i) an assumption on the tail of the distribution of $\ell(\theta, \xi)$ is required to find a confidence interval for the mean [Bahadur1956]; (ii) the scheme is simplified considerably. An example of a valid bound is $\ell_n(\theta) = \sup_{\xi \in \Xi} \, \ell(\theta, \xi)$.

We will use a data-driven proxy for the expectation by introducing permutation invariant ambiguity sets. These are subsets $\amb$ of the probability simplex $\Delta^n$ such that, for each $\mu \in \amb$ any permutation of $\mu$ is also in $\amb$. The ordered risk minimization problem is then: $$\minimize_{\theta \in \Theta} \quad \sup_{\mu \in \amb} \, \sum_{i=1}^{n} \mu_i \ell_i(\theta).$$ This proxy cost interpolates between the robust case for $\amb = \Delta^n$ and the sample average (including a term associated with $\ell_n(\theta)$) when $\amb = \{\one_n/n\}$. The interpolation interpretation is also common in DRO [Ben-Tal2013]. To find a good balance, our goal is to select $\amb$ such that, for all $\theta \in \Theta$, $$\prob\left[\sup_{\mu \in \amb} \, \sum_{i=1}^{n} \mu_i \ell_i(\theta) \geq \E[\ell(\theta, \xi)] \right] \geq 1 - \delta,$$ We refer to this problem as the calibration problem. It robustifies against disparities between the empirical and true distributions. The parameter $\delta$ then serves as an intuitive, user-determined parameter that controls the conservativeness of the method. However, as illustrated by experiments, our method is relatively insensitive to the value of $\delta$.

To find an ambiguity set $\amb$ satisfying eq:calibration we need to somehow parametrize it. A well known class of permutation invariant ambiguity sets uses $\phi$-divergences [Ben-Tal2013]. Let $\phi \colon \Re_+ \to \Re$ be lower semicontinuous, convex and $\phi = 0$. lower semicontinuous envelope of the terms inside the sum to handle cases where $\nu_i$ equals zero. $I_{\phi}(\mu, \nu) \dfn \ssum_{i=1}^{n} \nu_i \phi(\mu_i / \nu_i)$ for all $\mu, \nu \in \Delta^n$. A (centered) $\phi$-divergence ambiguity set is then $$\amb_{\alpha} \dfn \left\{ \mu \in \Delta^{n} \colon I_{\phi}\left(\mu, \frac{\one_{n}}{n}\right) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{\phi(n \mu_i)}{n} \leq \alpha \right\}.$$ In this work we consider two examples: total variation (TV) for which $\phi(t) = |t - 1|$ and Kullback Leibler (KL) divergence for which $\phi(t) = t \log t - t + 1$. However, our method works for any divergence. See [Ben-Tal2013]for more examples. $\amb_\alpha$ the radius $\alpha \in \Re$ should then be the smallest value such that eq:calibration still holds. We also provide an alternative parametrization, related to a well known bound by Anderson [Anderson1969]and the conditional value-at-risk.

It is important to note that the constraint in eq:calibration is less stringent compared to DRO, which guarantees that the supremum in eq:calibration acts as a high-confidence upper bound, uniformly over $\theta$ The mean bound will be uniform when After all, we never require that the true distribution is contained within $\amb$ (as is the case in [Beck2017]). The gap between the point-wise eq:calibration and the uniform equivalent is examined for $\phi$-divergences in [Duchi2021b,Lam2019]in the asymptotic regime.

For discontinuous cdf we first introduce a construction of the joint distribution of random vectors $W'$ and $\nu'$, such that $W' \deq W$ and $\nu' \deq \nu$ (i.e., the marginals are as specified in the lemma). Later we also provide an alternative parametrization similar to the conditional value-at-risk (cf. cor:simple-distortionand the discussion below).

Evaluating the right-hand side of eq:distortion-confidence was studied in the context of lower bounding the cdf of a random variable (cf. [Moscovich2020]for an efficient and numerically stable algorithm). $\sum_{i=1}^{n} \mu_i Z_{(i)}$ is called a distortion risk [Bertsimas2009b], which is a convex function of $(Z_1, \dots, Z_n)$ iff $\mu \in \Re^n_{\uparrow}$. Since the uniform order statistics form a uniform distribution over a convex set (i.e., $\Re^n_{\uparrow} \cap ^n$), their density is quasi-concave [Shapiro2021]. Hence we can leverage [Shapiro2021] to claim that the calibration problem $$\inf_{\mu \in \Re^n_{\uparrow} \cap \Delta^n} \, \left\{\varphi(\mu) \colon \prob\left[\sum_{i=1}^k \mu_i \leq U_{(k)}, \, \forall k \in [n]\right] \geq 1 - \delta \right\}$$ is a convex problem, whenever $\varphi$ is convex. We will study the choice of $\varphi$, which should measure the size of $\amb$, in future work as well as the tractability of the calibration problem. For now, we instead consider a specific parametrization: $$\mu^{(\gamma)}\dfn \left(0, \dots, 0, \tfrac{\lceil (n-1)\gamma \rceil}{n-1} - \gamma, \tfrac{1}{n-1}, \dots, \tfrac{1}{n-1}, \gamma\right),$$ with $\gamma \geq 1/(n-1)$, such that $\mu \in \Delta^n \cap \Re_\uparrow^n$ holds. Then $$\sum_{i=1}^{n} \mu_i^{(\gamma)} Z_{(i)} = \left(\tfrac{d}{n-1} - \gamma \right) Z_{(d)} + \sum_{i=d+1}^{n-1} \tfrac{Z_{(i)}}{n-1} + \gamma Z_{(n)},$$ with $d = \lceil (n-1)\gamma\rceil$. The final expression is a well-known bound for the expectation of $Z$ due to Anderson [Anderson1969]. The re-interpretation in terms of a distortion risk is novel to the authors' knowledge. In the technical report app:cvar we also show that the final expression is an affine transformation of the well-known conditional value-at-risk. Hence we denote it as $\bar{\CVAR}$ in the experiments. The value of $\gamma$ in the context of [Anderson1969] is usually determined using an asymptotic bound (cf. [Wilks1964]). We find an accurate value by solving: $$\inf_{\gamma \geq 1/(n-1)} \, \left\{\gamma \colon \prob\left[\sum_{i=1}^{k} \mu_i^{(\gamma)} \leq U_{(k)}, \, \forall k \in [n] \right] \geq 1- \delta\right\},$$ for a user-specified $\delta \in $ with a scalar root finderfoot:brentq. The probability is evaluated numerically using [Moscovich2020]. The calibration problem eq:calibration-cvar in [Anderson1969] is interpreted as moving the empirical cdf down as little as possible, while still guaranteeing that it lower bounds the true cdf with high probability. It is also comparable to producing the tightest mean bound as in the calibration problem eq:calibration-scalar.

In this work we consider expected risk minimization & \minimize_{\theta \in \Theta} &\quad& \E[\ell(\theta, \xi)].

Here $\Theta \subseteq \Re^{n_\theta}$ and $\xi \colon \Omega \to \Xi \subseteq \Re^{n_{\xi}}$ a random vector defined on sample space $(\Omega, \F, \prob)$. Problems like eq:expected-risk-minimization are omnipresent in machine-learning and statistics [Shalev-Shwartz2013,Vapnik1998].

In practice the expectation in eq:expected-risk-minimization cannot be evaluated exactly. Instead a sample $\{\xi_i\}_{i=1}^n$ is available. Let $\ell_i(\theta) = \ell(\theta, \xi_i)$ and $L(\theta) = (\ell_1(\theta), \dots, \ell_{n}(\theta))$.

& \minimize_{\theta \in \Theta} \, \maximize_{\mu \in \amb} &\quad& \<\mu, L(\theta)\>, for some convex, non-empty and closed ambiguity set $\amb \subset \Re^n$ of probability vectors. The usual empirical risk minimization or sample average approach (SAA) amounts to $\amb = \{\one_n/n\}$. We denote the value of the maximization in eq:robustified-erm as $\rho[L(\theta)]$ and refer to it as a risk measure [Shapiro2021]. When $\amb$ is permutation invariant (i.e. for any $\mu \in \amb$ and permutation $\pi$, $\pi\mu \in \amb$) we show $$\prob\left[\rho[L(\theta)] \dfn \sup_{\mu \in \amb} \<\mu, L(\theta)\> \geq \E[\ell(\theta, \xi)]\right] \geq 1 - \varepsilon$$ for all $\theta \in \Theta$ when $\ell_n(\theta)$ is replaced by $\bar{\ell}(\theta)$ which upper bounds $\ell(\theta, \xi)$ with probability one. The value of $1 - \varepsilon$ is given by the volume of a so-called distortion set, which is related to $\rho$ through a modified convex conjugate. Whenever $\rho$ satisfies eq:well-callibrated we say it is $\varepsilon$-calibrated. Note that eq:well-callibrated defines two problems: (i) an analysis problem, where given some $\rho$ a $\varepsilon$ should be determined; (ii) a synthesis/calibration problem where we want to determine some (parametric) $\rho$ such that eq:well-callibrated holds for some user provided $\varepsilon$. We solve both problems in this work and it turns out that calibrated risk measures solving (ii) serve as good proxy costs for eq:expected-risk-minimization. eq:robustified-erm instead of a sample average is nothing new and several sets $\amb$ have been considered under the framework of distributionally robust optimization (DRO) [Shapiro2021]. Specifically $\amb$ can be based on moments as in [Delage2010], divergence from the empirical distribution as in [Ben-Tal2013], Wasserstein distance [Esfahani2018b] or other statistical hypothesis tests [Bertsimas2018]. See [Lin2022,Rahimian2019] for recent surveys. There are however some issues with classical DRO. Sets often depend on some parameters (e.g. some radius). These are sometimes determined using concentration inequalities [Delage2010] or using inexact asymptotic bounds [Ben-Tal2013] to make sure the true distribution is contained within $\amb$ with high confidence. This can often be conservative in the case fo concentration inequalities, either due to loose constants in the concentration inequalities or due to the shape of the ambiguity set. So instead one often resorts to either bootstrapping or cross validation (cf. [Esfahani2018b]), which can be computationally expensive and lack statistical guarantees for finite samples, similarly to the asymptotic bounds.

Recently, in the asymptotic setting, this conservativeness has been tackled by looking at cost-aware bounds like eq:well-callibrated [Duchi2021b,Lam2019] with good results. They also discuss the gap between eq:well-callibrated and the usual uniform bounds (i.e. $\prob[\rho[L(\theta)] \geq \E[\ell(\theta, \xi)], \forall \theta] \geq 1 - \varepsilon'$) provided by DRO. The finite sample case has not been considered much. We take the first steps in that direction in this work.

To summarize, our approach is novel as follows:

- Our scheme provides cost-aware, point-wise statistical guarantees as in eq:well-callibrated for any number of samples; - The bounds are tight for any distribution, replacing the involved analytical derivations of concentration inequalities with efficient numerical procedures; - The framework applies to many risks from literature, including distortion [Bertsimas2009b] and $\phi$-divergence risks [Ben-Tal2013]; - We present the distortion representation of risks.

The resulting scheme also outperforms SAA in both regression and classification as illustrated in sec:case-studies, while being less sensitive to tuning parameters.

We exploit results from convex duality [Rockafellar1998], order statistics [Wilks1964,David2003], Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistics [Moscovich2020], majorization [Marshall2011,Steerneman1990] and isotonic regression [Best2000].

The rest of the paper proceeds as follows. In sec:statistical-framework we derive mean bounds linked to law-invariant coherent risk measures through the distortion representation in sec:distortion-representation. The problem of calibrating risk measures given $\varepsilon$ is answered in sec:calibration and in sec:case-studiesnumerical case studies are presented, showing the performance of these calibrated risks.

### Distortion risk

We consider simple distortion sets, which are generated by one vector. That is $\distort = \mu_\uparrow + (\M^n)^\circ$ with $\mu_{\uparrow} \in \Delta^n \cap \M^n$. fig:graphical-equivalence depicts one such set. Simple distortion sets correspond to distortion risk measures (also called spectral risk measures [Shapiro2021], [Pflug2007]) which are law-invariant, coherent and comonotonic [Bertsimas2009b].

For simple distortion sets $\distort = \mu_{\uparrow} + (\M^n)^\circ$ with $\mu \in \Delta^n$, the associated risk measure $\rho$ given by eq:distortion-representation is a distortion risk measure [Bertsimas2009b] and $$\rho(X) = \<\mu_{\uparrow}, X_{\uparrow}\>.$$ The coverage probability of simple distortion sets is expressed in terms of order statistics.

For simple distortion sets $\distort = \mu_{\uparrow} + (\M^n)^\circ$ with $\mu \in \Delta^n$, the coverage probability $$\prob[W \in \distort] = \prob\left[\sum_{i=1}^{k} \mu_{(i)} \leq U_{(k)}, \, \forall k \in [n-1]\right],$$ where $U_{} \leq U_{} \leq \dots \leq U_{(n-1)}$ are order statistics associated with the uniform distribution over $$.

Bounds like the one in lem:order-statistics-bound have been extensively studied due to the link with the uniform empirical process and Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistics [Shorack2009]. An efficient method for the computation of eq:order-statistics-bound is given in [Moscovich2020].

We now present an example of a distortion risk that can be calibrated efficiently.

For $\gamma \in $, consider the following: $$\bar{\CVAR}^\gamma_n[X] = \left(\tfrac{d}{n-1} - \gamma \right) X_{(d)} + \sum_{i=d+1}^{n-1} \tfrac{X_{(i)}}{n-1} + \gamma X_{(n)},$$ which is a distortion risk with $d = \lceil (n-1)\gamma\rceil$ and $\gamma \geq 1/(n-1)$. It is a known bound for the mean [Anderson1969], relating directly to a one-sided Kolmogorov-Smirnov bound. In app:calibraton we also establish a novel link with the conditional value-at-risk, explaining the notation $\bar{\CVAR}$.

The exact method described in rem:order-statistics-bound is applicable to eq:biased-cvar and enables computing $\varepsilon$ for a given $\gamma$. This dependency is monotone, with $\gamma = 0, 1$ giving $\varepsilon = 1, 0$ respectively. So scalar root finders We use brentq as implemented in scipy1.10.0 can find $\gamma$ for a given value of $\varepsilon$.

Alternatively, asymptotic bounds for eq:order-statistics-bound exist in literature. For sufficiently large $n$ [Wilks1964] $$\varepsilon = e^{-2 (n-1) \gamma^2} \quad \Leftrightarrow \quad \gamma = \sqrt{\tfrac{\log(1/\varepsilon)}{2n - 2}}.$$

### $\phi$-divergence risk

The $\phi$-divergence [Ben-Tal2013] between $\mu, \nu \in \Delta^n$ is defined as $$I_{\phi}(\mu, \nu) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \nu_i \phi\left(\mu_i/\nu_i \right)$$ where $\phi(t)$ is convex for $t \geq 0$, $0\phi(a/0) \dfn a \lim_{t\to\infty} \phi(t)/t$, $0\phi(0/0) \dfn 0$ and $\phi = 0$. A (centered) $\phi$-divergence ambiguity set is then $$\amb_{\alpha}^n \dfn \left\{ \mu \colon I_{\phi}^n(\mu) \dfn I_{\phi}(\mu, \one_n/n) \leq \alpha\right\},$$ with $\one_n/n \dfn (1/n, \dots, 1/n)$. The distortion set associated with it is $\distort_{\alpha}^n \dfn \amb_{\alpha}^n + (\M^n)^\circ$, by thm:distortion-representation. The set $\amb_{\alpha}^n$ is clearly permutation invariant, since [eq:phi-divergence] $\phi$-divergence risk corresponds to solving $$\argmin_{\alpha \geq 0} \left\{ \alpha \colon \prob\left[W \in \distort_{\alpha}^n \right] \geq 1 - \epsilon \right\}.$$ Unlike in the previous section, the probability cannot be evaluated exactly. Instead we compute a sample approximation to eq:scenario-programas follows: Let $\{W^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^m$ be an sample uniformly from $\Delta^n$ and $\alpha_i \dfn ({I_{\phi}^n}^*)^\diamond(W^{(i)})$ for $i \in [m]$ with associated order statistics $\alpha_{} \leq \dots \leq \alpha_{(m)}$. Then, for some $\epsilon \in $ $$\prob\left[\prob\left[({I_{\phi}^n}^*)^\diamond(W) \leq \alpha_{(u)} \mid W^{(i)} \right] \geq 1 - \varepsilon \right] \geq 1 - \beta$$ with $\beta = I_{1-\varepsilon}(u, m-u+1)$ the regularized incomplete beta function (i.e., the cdf of a beta distribution) at level $1-\epsilon$. $m$ values of $\alpha_i$ and sort them. For given $\beta$ and $\varepsilon$, take $u \in [n]$ such that $\beta = I_{1-\varepsilon}(u, m-u+1)$ or increase $m$ if no such $u$ exists. Then thm:divergence-bound says $\rho$ with radius $\alpha_{(u)}$ is $\varepsilon$-calibrated with probability at least $1-\beta$.

Note that eq:scenario-program can be viewed as a chance-constrained program. So instead of relying on classical bounds from order statistics as we did in the proof of thm:distortion-bound in app:calibraton, we could use [Campi2018]. We evaluate $({I_{\phi}^n}^*)^\diamond(W)$ with a modified pool adjacent violators (PAV) algorithm, which has worst-case computational complexity of $\mathcal{O}(n)$ [Best2000]. Details are provided in rem:pav.

## Preliminaries

### Monotone Cone

Let $\Re^n_{\uparrow} \dfn \{x \in \Re^n \colon x_1 \leq x_2 \leq \dots \leq x_n\}$ denote the monotone cone. This cone and its polar have a history in isotonic regression [Barlow1972] and majorization [Steerneman1990]. $\major{n}$ and $\Re_\uparrow^n$ are related.

Let $\Re_\uparrow^n$ be the monotone cone and $\major{n}$ as in eq:majorization-cone. Then $\major{n}$ is the polar of $\Re_\uparrow^n$.

The monotone cone is polyhedral with $\Re_\uparrow^n = \{x \colon Mx \leq 0\}$ for $M \in \Re^{n-1 \times n}$ with $Mx = (x_1 - x_2, x_2 - x_3, \dots, x_{n-1} - x_n)$. The definition of the polar cone is thus (\Re_\uparrow^n)^\circ &= \left\{ y \in \Re^n \colon \<x, y\> \leq 0, \forall x \text{ s.t. } M x \leq 0 \right\}.

By Farkas' lemma [Boyd2004] we have either $Mx \leq 0$ and $\<x, y\> > 0$ or $\trans{M} \lambda = y$ and $\lambda \geq 0$.

(\Re_\uparrow^n)^\circ = \left\{ y \in \Re^n \colon y = \trans{M} \lambda, \lambda \geq 0 \right\}.

Note that $\<\lambda, Mx\> = \sum_{i=1}^{n-1} \lambda_i (x_i - x_{i+1}) = \sum_{j=1}^{n} x_j (\lambda_{j} - \lambda_{j-1}) = \<\trans{M} \lambda, x\>$, where $\lambda_0 = \lambda_{n} = 0$. Thus $y \in (\Re_\uparrow^n)^\circ$ iff $y = \trans{M} \lambda$ for $\lambda \geq 0$. Here $y = \trans{M} \lambda$ holds iff y_j &= \lambda_j - \lambda_{j-1}, &&\forall j \in [n]. \\\Leftrightarrow \quad \ssum_{j=1}^k y_j &= \ssum_{j=1}^k \lambda_j - \lambda_{j-1} = \lambda_k, &&\forall k \in [n].

Since $\lambda \geq 0$ we have $\sum_{j=1}^k y_j = \lambda_k \geq 0$ for $k \in [n-1]$ and $\sum_{j=1}^{n} y_j = \lambda_n = 0$. These are the constraints in eq:majorization-cone.

### Details on $\bar{\CVAR}$

We first characterize the conditional value-at-risk in terms of order statistics as a distortion risk below.

Consider $\CVAR_{n}^{\gamma} \colon \Re^{n} \to \Re$ as $$\CVAR_{n}^{\gamma}[X] = \inf_{\tau} \left\{ \tau + \frac{1}{(1-\gamma) n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} [X_i - \tau]_+ \right\},$$ for $\gamma \in $. Then $$(1 - \gamma)\CVAR_n^{\gamma}[X] = \left(\frac{d}{n} - \gamma \right) X_{(d)} + \sum_{i=d+1}^{n} \frac{X_{(i)}}{n},$$ with $d \dfn \lceil n\gamma \rceil$. So $\CVAR_n^\gamma$ is a distortion risk.

Consider the minimizers in the definition of $\CVAR$: $$\argmin_\tau \left\{ \tau + \frac{1}{(1-\gamma) n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} [X_i - \tau]_+ \right\}.$$ By [Rockafellar2000], this set is a closed bounded interval with the left endpoint being \VAR_{n}^{\gamma}[X] &\dfn \inf_x \{x \colon F_n(x) \geq \gamma\} \\&= \inf_x \left\{ x \colon \sum_{i=1}^{n }\bm{1}_{(-\infty, x]}(X_i) \geq \gamma n \right\} = X_{(d)}, with $F_n$ the empirical cdf, the definition of which we plugged in for the second equality. For the third equality note that the left-hand side counts the number of values $X_i$ smaller than or equal to $x$.

$$X_{(d-k-1)} < X_{(d-k)} = X_{(d-k+1)} = \dots = X_{(d)},$$ for $k \geq 0$. Then clearly there are at least $d = \lceil n \gamma \rceil > n\gamma$ values smaller than or equal to $X_{(d-k)}$. For any $z < X_{(d-k)}$ there are at most $d-k-1$ samples values than or equal. Hence $\VAR_{n}^{\gamma}[X] = X_{(d-k)} = X_{(d)}$.

Plugging into the cost of &X_{(d)} + \frac{1}{(1-\gamma) n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} [X_i - X_{(d)}]_+ \nonumber \\where we used $X_{(i)} \leq X_{(d)}$ for $i \leq d$. The stated result follows from some basic algebraic manipulation. Finally note that $d/n - \gamma = (\lceil n\gamma \rceil - n\gamma)/n \leq 1/n$. So the monotonicity constraint on $\mu$ in cor:simple-distortion is also satisfied.

We can then rewrite $$\bar{\CVAR}_n^\gamma[X] \dfn (1 - \gamma) \CVAR_{n-1}^{\gamma}[(X_{(i)})_{i=1}^{n-1}] + \gamma X_{(n)}.$$ The associated distortion risk has weights $\mu^{(\gamma)}$ as in eq:cvar-weights. So we need $\gamma \geq 1/(n-1)$ for $\mu \in \Delta^n \cap \Re_\uparrow^n$ to hold. The advantage of $\bar{\CVAR}$ as a well-calibrated risk measure over $\CVAR$ is that additional weight is placed on the largest sample. This often makes the mean bound associated with $\bar{\CVAR}$ less conservative compared to $\CVAR$ for the same confidence level.

Let $\Re^n_{\uparrow} \dfn \{x \in \Re^n \colon x_1 \leq x_2 \leq \dots \leq x_n\}$ denote the monotone cone. This cone and its dual have a history in isotonic regression [Barlow1972] and majorization [Steerneman1990].

We derive the polar of Let $\M^n$ be the monotone cone.

(\M^n)^\circ = \left\{ x \in \Re^n \colon \sum_{i=1}^k x_i \geq 0, \forall k \in [n-1], \sum_{i=1}^n x_i = 0 \right\}.

The monotone cone is polyhedral with $\M^n = \{x \colon Mx \leq 0\}$ for $M \in \Re^{n-1 \times n}$ with $Mx = (x_1 - x_2, x_2 - x_3, \dots, x_{n-1} - x_n)$. The definition of the polar cone is thus (\M^n)^\circ &= \left\{ y \in \Re^n \colon \<x, y\> \leq 0, \forall x \text{ s.t. } M x \leq 0 \right\}.

By Farkas' lemma [Boyd2004] we have either $Mx \leq 0$ and $\<x, y\> > 0$ or $\trans{M} \lambda = y$ and $\lambda \geq 0$.

(\M^n)^\circ = \left\{ y \in \Re^n \colon y = \trans{M} \lambda, \lambda \geq 0 \right\}.

Note that $\<\lambda, Mx\> = \sum_{i=1}^{n-1} \lambda_i (x_i - x_{i+1}) = \sum_{j=1}^{n} x_j (\lambda_{j} - \lambda_{j-1}) = \<\trans{M} \lambda, x\>$, where $\lambda_0 = \lambda_{n} = 0$. Thus $y \in (\M^n)^\circ$ iff $y = \trans{M} \lambda$ for $\lambda \geq 0$. Here $y = \trans{M} \lambda$ holds iff y_j &= \lambda_j - \lambda_{j-1}, &&\forall j \in [n]. \\\Leftrightarrow \quad \ssum_{j=1}^k y_j &= \ssum_{j=1}^k \lambda_j - \lambda_{j-1} = \lambda_k, &&\forall k \in [n].

Since $\lambda \geq 0$ we have $\sum_{j=1}^k y_j = \lambda_k \geq 0$ for $k \in [n-1]$ and $\sum_{j=1}^{n} y_j = \lambda_n = 0$.

$$x_{\uparrow} \in y_{\uparrow} + (\M^n)^\circ \quad \text{iff} \quad x \slt y,$$ where $x \slt y$ denotes that $x$ is majorized by $y$.

The definition of majorization [Marshall2011] is: x \slt y \, \Leftrightarrow \, \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_{(i)} \geq \sum_{i=1}^{n} y_{(i)} \text{ and } \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i = \sum_{i=1}^{n} y_i.

This is clearly equivalent to prop:dual-monotone-cone applied to $x_{\uparrow} - y_{\uparrow}$.

Majorization is related to the convex hull of the orbit under $\Pi^n$. Specifically [Marshall2011]: $$x \slt y \quad \Leftrightarrow \quad x \in \hull(\Pi^n y).$$ This has the following useful consequence.

$$\hull (\Pi^n y) = \bigcap_{\pi \in \Pi^n} \pi(y_{\uparrow} + (\M^n)^\circ).$$ The result is a specialization of [Steerneman1990]. Take $x \in \hull (\Pi^n y)$. Then $x \slt y$ by eq:permuto-hull-eq and $x_{\uparrow} - y_{\uparrow} \in (\M^n)^\circ$ by prop:group-induced-cone-order. Using the definition of the polar cone this gives: &\<x_{\uparrow} - y_{\uparrow}, u_{\uparrow}\> \leq 0, &&\forall u_{\uparrow} \in \M^n \\\Leftrightarrow \quad &\<x_{\uparrow}, u_{\uparrow}\> \leq \<y_{\uparrow}, u_{\uparrow}\>, &&\forall u_{\uparrow} \in \M^n \\\Rightarrow \quad &\<x, u_{\uparrow}\> \leq \<y_{\uparrow}, u_{\uparrow}\>, &&\forall u_{\uparrow} \in \M^n, \\\Leftrightarrow \quad &x - y_{\uparrow} \in (\M^n)^\circ, where we used $\<x, u_{\uparrow}\> \leq \<x_{\uparrow}, u_{\uparrow}\>$ [Marshall2011] for the third implication and the definition of the polar cone again for the fourth. Thus we have shown that $x \in y_{\uparrow} + (\M^n)^\circ$. As we could have taken any $x \in \hull(\Pi^n y)$ we thus showed $\hull (\Pi^n y) \subseteq y_{\uparrow} + (\M^n)^\circ$. We can take permutations of both sides without affecting the result (since permutations are invertible). Moreover $\pi \hull(\Pi^n y) = \hull(\Pi^n y)$. Thus $\hull (\Pi^n y) \subseteq \pi(y_{\uparrow} + (\M^n)^\circ)$ for all $\pi \in \Pi^n$ and $\hull (\Pi^n y) \subseteq \cap_{\pi} \pi(y_{\uparrow} + (\M^n)^\circ)$.

For the converse let $x \in \cap_{\pi \in \Pi^n} \pi(y_{\uparrow} + (\M^n)^\circ)$. So $x_{\uparrow} \in y_{\uparrow} + (\M^n)^\circ$. By prop:group-induced-cone-order we have $x \slt y$. Applying eq:permuto-hull-eq then implies $x \in \hull(\Pi^n y)$. Therefore $\cap_{\pi \in \Pi^n} \pi(y_{\uparrow} + (\M^n)^\circ) \subseteq \hull(\Pi^n y) $, completing the proof.

We can generalize the above to apply to convex, permutation invariant sets.

Consider some convex permutation invariant $\set{A} \subseteq \Re^n$ (i.e. $x \in \set{A}$ implies $\pi x \in \set{A}$ for all $\pi \in \Pi^n$).

$$\set{A} = \bigcap_{\pi \in \Pi^n} \pi(\set{A} + (\M^n)^{\circ}).$$ Let $x \in \set{A}$. Since the identity map is also in $\Pi^n$, prop:permuto-hull implies $\hull(\Pi^n x) \subseteq x_{\uparrow} + (\Re^n_{\uparrow})^\circ$. Therefore we have $\pi(x) \in x_{\uparrow} + (\M^n)^\circ$ for all $\pi \in \Pi^n$. Noting that $x_{\uparrow} \in \set{A}$ by permutation invariance gives $\pi(x) \in \set{A} + (\Re^n_{\uparrow})^\circ$ for all $\pi \in \Pi^n$. For every $\pi \in \Pi^n$, also $\pi^{-1} \in \Pi^n$. We can apply this inverse to both sides of the inclusion. Thus $x \in \cap_{\pi} \pi(\set{A} + (\M^n)^\circ)$ and $\set{A} \subseteq \cap_{\pi} \pi(\set{A} + (\M^n)^\circ)$.

For the converse let $x \in \cap_{\pi} \pi(\set{A} + (\M^n)^\circ)$. Then, again since $\pi^{-1} \in \Pi^n$, $\pi(x) \in \set{A} + (\M^n)^\circ$ for all $\pi \in \Pi^n$. Specifically $x_{\uparrow} \in \set{A} + (\M^n)^\circ$, which implies $\exists y \in \set{A}$ such that $x_{\uparrow} \in y + (\M^n)^\circ$. Note that $y \in y_{\uparrow} + (\M^n)^\circ$ by prop:permuto-hull. That is $\exists s \in (\M^n)^\circ$ such that $y = y_{\uparrow} + s$ and $x_{\uparrow} \in y + s + (\M^n)^\circ$. Since $(\M^n)^\circ$ is a convex cone, $s + s' \in (\M^n)^\circ$ for $s, s' \in (\M^n)^\circ$. Thus $x_{\uparrow} \in y_{\uparrow} + (\M^n)^\circ$, which by prop:group-induced-cone-order implies $x \slt y$ or $x \in \hull(\Pi^n y)$ eq:permuto-hull-eq. From $y \in \set{A}$ and permutation invariance $\Pi^n y \subseteq \set{A}$. Moreover, by convexity, $\hull(\Pi^n y) \subseteq \set{A}$. Thus $x \in \set{A}$ and $\cap_{\pi} \pi(\set{A} + (\M^n)^\circ) \subseteq \set{A}$.

We finish with a characterization of the convex hull of the permutations of some vector as a polyhedral set.

Consider a vector $x \in \Re^n$ with $d$ distinct elements, which we store in $\bar{x} \in \Re^d$. Let $c \in \N^d$ denote the number of copies of each component of $\bar{x}$ in $x$. Then \hull\left(\Pi^n x \right) = \{ S\bar{x} \colon S\one_d = \one_n, \trans{\one}_n S = \trans{c}, S \geq 0\}.
