Visual Sensitivities to Color Differences in Daylight*

Topics include MacAdam ellipses, Color difference, Colorimetry, Visual perception, Chromaticity, CIE color space, Psychophysics.

MacAdam experimentally maps just-noticeable chromaticity differences under daylight conditions and represents the results as ellipses in the 1931 I.C.I. chromaticity diagram. The paper is the source of MacAdam ellipses, a compact way to show that perceptual color distance is strongly direction- and location-dependent in standard chromaticity coordinates.

An apparatus is described which facilitates the presentation of pairs of variable colors without variation of luminance. With this instrument, various criteria of visual sensitivity to color difference have been investigated. The standard deviation of color matching was finally adopted as the most reproducible criterion. The test field was two degrees in diameter, divided by a vertical biprism edge, and was viewed centrally with a surrounding field of forty-two degrees diameter uniformly illuminated so as to have a chromaticity similar to that of the I.C.I. Standard Illuminant C (average daylight). The luminance of the test field was maintained constant at 15 millilamberts, and the surrounding field was 7.5 millilamberts. These fields were viewed monocularly through an artificial pupil, 2.6 mm in diameter. Over twenty-five thousand trials at color matching have been recorded for a single observer, and the readings are analyzed in detail and compared with previously available data. The standard deviations of the trials are represented in terms of distance in the standard 1931 I.C.I. chromaticity diagram....
