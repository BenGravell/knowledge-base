Concerning Nonnegative Matrices and Doubly Stochastic Matrices

Topics include Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm, Matrix scaling, Doubly stochastic matrices, Nonnegative matrices, Alternating normalization, Matrix analysis.

<!-- chunk {"id": "summary-0002", "role": "summary", "section": "Summary", "weight": 2.0} -->

Sinkhorn and Knopp characterize when alternating row and column scaling of a nonnegative matrix converges to a doubly stochastic limit. The paper extends Sinkhorn's positive-matrix scaling result to nonnegative matrices and supplies core conditions behind the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm used in matrix balancing and entropic optimal transport.

<!-- chunk {"id": "abstract-0003", "role": "abstract", "section": "Abstract", "weight": 2.0} -->

This paper is concerned with the condition for the convergence to a doubly stochastic limit of a sequence of matrices obtained from a nonnegative matrix A by alternately scaling the rows and columns of A and with the condition for the existence of diagonal matrices D1 and D2 with positive main diagonals such that D1AD2 is doubly stochastic. The result is the following. The sequence of matrices converges to a doubly stochastic limit if and only if the matrix A contains at least one positive diagonal. A necessary and sufficient condition that there exist diagonal matrices D1 and D2 with positive main diagonals such that D1AD2 is both doubly stochastic and the limit of the iteration is that A is not the zero matrix and each positive entry of A is contained in a positive diagonal. The form D1AD2 is unique, and D1 and D2 are unique up to a positive scalar multiple if and only if A is fully indecomposable.
