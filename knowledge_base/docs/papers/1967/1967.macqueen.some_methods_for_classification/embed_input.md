Some Methods for Classification and Analysis of Multivariate Observations

Topics include k-means, Clustering, Multivariate analysis, Unsupervised learning, Classification, Vector quantization, Large samples.

MacQueen introduces k-means as a practical method for partitioning multivariate observations into k groups with low within-class variance. The paper gives both computational motivation and asymptotic analysis, making it an origin point for one of the most widely used clustering algorithms in statistics and machine learning.

The main purpose of this paper is to describe a process for partitioning an N-dimensional population into k sets on the basis of a sample. The process, which is called 'k-means,' appears to give partitions which are reasonably efficient in the sense of within-class variance. That is, if p is the probability mass function for the population, S = {S_1, S_2,..., S_k} is a partition of E_N, and u_i, i = 1, 2,..., k, is the conditional mean of p over the set S_i, then w^2(S) = sum from i = 1 to k of integral over S_i of |z - u_i|^2 dp(z) tends to be low for the partitions S generated by the method. We say 'tends to be low,' primarily because of intuitive considerations, corroborated to some extent by mathematical analysis and practical computational experience. Also, the k-means procedure is easily programmed and is computationally economical, so that it is feasible to process very large samples on a digital computer. Possible applications include methods for similarity grouping, nonlinear prediction, approximating multivariate distributions, and nonparametric tests for independence among several variables....
