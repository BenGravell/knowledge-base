## Introduction

Learning the dynamical behavior of nonlinear systems is an important and challenging problem with applications ranging from engineering, physics, biology \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib7), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib56), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib5)\], to language modeling, and sequence predictions \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib26), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib2)\]. Bilinear dynamical systems (BLDS) constitute a simple yet powerful class of nonlinear systems naturally arising in a variety of domains from engineering, biology \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib36)\], quantum mechanical processes \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib41)\] to recommendation systems \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib27)\]. Moreover, bilinear systems approximate a much broader class of nonlinear systems via Carleman linearization \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib28)\] or Koopman canonical transform \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib17), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib3)\] of control-affine nonlinear systems \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib55), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib31)\]. Therefore, learning the dynamics of BLDS from input-output data is an important and useful problem which has attracted significant interest, both in the case of continuous-time \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib23), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib57)\] and discrete-time \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib4)\]. However, theoretical guarantees of learning BLDS from a single trajectory of noisy input-output data is lacking, with current guarantees existing only for bilinear systems with complete state observations \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib50), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib9)\]. Our goal in this paper is to provide an algorithm and theoretical guarantees for learning partially observed BLDS from noisy input-output data sampled from a single trajectory. We achieve this by learning the system's *Markov-like parameters*, which uniquely identify the end-to-end behavior of the system, and can be used to recover the state-space matrices up to a similarity transform using existing algorithms \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib18), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib52), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib39)\].

Our goal relates to the problem of learning linear dynamical system (LDS) from partial state-observations. In this setting, a line of recent work focuses on finite sample error bounds. \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib58), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib39), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib43), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib49), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib10), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib60), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib54), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib6)\] study methods which use least squares regression to learn the system's Markov parameters or Hankel matrix, which can then be used to recover the state-space matrices (up to a similarity transform) using classic Ho-Kalman Algorithm \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib18)\]. \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib48), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib49), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib15)\] study system identification with Hankel nuclear norm regularization. Other works have focused on learning to predict the behavior of partially observed LDS via gradient descent \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib19)\] and spectral filtering \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib20)\]. The linear setting has been extended to (decode-able) nonlinear \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib32)\] observations and bilinear \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib45)\] partial observations. However, to the best of our knowledge, sample complexity and non-asymptotic analysis for partially observed nonlinear dynamical systems (including BLDS) have not been considered before.

Non-asymptotic learning of (non)linear dynamical systems with complete state observations has also attracted significant attention recently. Most of the advancements in this direction are focused on linear systems \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib16), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib11), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib46), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib12), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib14), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib54), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib51), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib30), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib22), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib63)\], where an optimal error rate is achieved by using either mixing-time \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib64)\] or martingale-based arguments \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib1)\]. These results have been extended to switched linear dynamical systems \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib53), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib44), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib13), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib42)\], as well as certain classes of nonlinear dynamical systems, including state transition models with nonlinear activation functions \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib40), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib8), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib32), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib47), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib21)\], nonlinear features \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib34), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib33)\], or from a nonparametric perspective \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib59), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib65), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib29)\]. However, the problem of learning nonlinear dynamical systems from partial observations of a single trajectory is still an open problem. In this paper, we take a step towards addressing this problem by answering the following question:

*Can we learn a partially observed bilinear dynamical system from a single trajectory?*

The main difficulty arises from the fact that the hidden states evolve according to a bilinear state equation, for which the analysis tools developed for learning partially observed LDS do not work. Moreover, the stability of a BLDS explicitly depends on the input sequence, which is in stark contrast to the deterministic notion of stability in the case of LDS.

Contributions: We overcome the aforementioned challenges and provide theoretical guarantees for learning partially observed bilinear dynamical systems. We make the following contributions towards bilinear system identification:

Sample complexity and error bounds: We provide the first sample complexity analysis and finite-sample error bounds for learning a realization of a partially observed BLDS from a single trajectory of input-output data. Unlike LDS, the output of a bilinear system maps to the history of inputs via nonlinear features (obtained by the Kronecker products of past inputs) and a sequence of Markov-like parameters with exponentially increasing length. Our main result (Theorem (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#Thmtheorem1 "Theorem 1 (Learning Markov-like parameters). ‣ 3 Learning the Markov-like Parameters ‣ Finite Sample Identification of Partially Observed Bilinear Dynamical Systems")) provides $\overset{\sim}{\mathcal{O}}{({1/\sqrt{T}})}$ error rate for learning these parameters from a single trajectory of length $T$. Our sample complexity bound $\overset{\sim}{\Omega}\left( {({p + 1})}^{L + 1} \right)$ grows exponentially with the history length $L$ (where $p$ is the input dimension), which correctly captures the dependence on the number of unknown Markov-like parameters (growing exponentially with $L$). For stable bilinear systems (defined in §[2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#S2.SS2 "2.2 Input Choice & Stability of Bilinear Dynamical Systems ‣ 2 Preliminaries ‣ Finite Sample Identification of Partially Observed Bilinear Dynamical Systems")), this can be mitigated by choosing a smaller history of inputs (see § (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#S5 "5 Numerical Experiments ‣ Finite Sample Identification of Partially Observed Bilinear Dynamical Systems")).

Input choice and stability: Stability of BLDS is typically input dependent. We work with a novel notion of stability (Definition (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#Thmdefinition1 "Definition 1 ((𝒰,𝜅,𝜌)-uniform-stability). ‣ Uniform stability in bilinear dynamical systems: ‣ 2.2 Input Choice & Stability of Bilinear Dynamical Systems ‣ 2 Preliminaries ‣ Finite Sample Identification of Partially Observed Bilinear Dynamical Systems")) that generalizes the classic notion of stability for LDS to the BLDS. We also define a notion of stability radius which governs our choice of inputs.

Persistence of excitation: Of independent interest, we establish the persistence of excitation (Theorem (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#Thmtheorem2 "Theorem 2 (Persistence of Excitation). ‣ 4.1 Persistence of Excitation ‣ 4 Sample Complexity Analysis ‣ Finite Sample Identification of Partially Observed Bilinear Dynamical Systems")) for a broader class of inputs (possibly heavy-tailed) satisfying a hyper-contractivity condition. Our persistence of excitation result holds for nonlinear, correlated, and heavy-tailed covariates (i.e., the input features).

Numerical experiments: Lastly, we perform experiments with synthetic data to verify our theoretical findings. Interestingly, our experiments show that exciting the system with inputs sampled uniformly at random from a sphere leads to better estimation of Markov-like parameters as compared to Gaussian inputs, empirically reinforcing our theory on choice of inputs.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows: §(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#S2 "2 Preliminaries ‣ Finite Sample Identification of Partially Observed Bilinear Dynamical Systems") sets up the problem and introduces the notion of stability. §(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#S3 "3 Learning the Markov-like Parameters ‣ Finite Sample Identification of Partially Observed Bilinear Dynamical Systems") provides our main result on learning Markov-like parameters of partially observed BLDS. §(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#S4 "4 Sample Complexity Analysis ‣ Finite Sample Identification of Partially Observed Bilinear Dynamical Systems") discusses our proof idea, and provides persistence of excitation result for a broader class of inputs. Lastly, we perform numerical experiments in §(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#S5 "5 Numerical Experiments ‣ Finite Sample Identification of Partially Observed Bilinear Dynamical Systems"), and conclude with a discussion of future directions in §(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#S6 "6 Conclusion and Future Direction ‣ Finite Sample Identification of Partially Observed Bilinear Dynamical Systems").

Then, observe that the products of matrices $\prod_{k = 0}^{\ell - 1}{({{\mathbf{u}}_{t - k} \circ {\mathbf{A}}})}$ may grow exponentially in norm if we consistently choose large inputs. This is precisely why stability in bilinear dynamical systems is more challenging than other classes of systems such as linear dynamical systems or switched systems.

Traditionally, notions like Mean Square Stability (MSS) have been considered to reason about the stability behavior of bilinear systems \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib24), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib41), (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib50)\]. Typically, these notions are asymptotic in nature, require distributional assumptions on the inputs, permit diverging trajectories with nonzero probability, and may not allow us to obtain tight guarantees. We introduce an alternative notion of stability that naturally generalizes the classical notion of stability in standard LTI systems.

### Definition 1 ($(\mathcal{U},\kappa,\rho)$-uniform-stability)

Let $\mathcal{U} \subseteq {\mathbb{R}}^{p}$, $\kappa \geq 1$, and $0 < \rho < 1$. We say that a partially observed bilinear dynamical system (as defined in ([2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#S2.E1 "Equation 2.1 ‣ 2.1 Problem Formulation ‣ 2 Preliminaries ‣ Finite Sample Identification of Partially Observed Bilinear Dynamical Systems"))) with state-transition matrices $\mathbf{A}:={\{\mathbf{A}_{0},\ldots,\mathbf{A}_{p}\}}$ is $(\mathcal{U},\kappa,\rho)$-uniformly-stable, if the joint spectral radius of the set $\mathcal{U} \circ \mathbf{A}$ satisfies: (i) ${\rho{({\mathcal{U} \circ \mathbf{A}})}} < \rho < 1$; and (ii) ${\phi{({\mathcal{U} \circ \mathbf{A}},\rho)}} \leq \kappa$.

Observe that if there exists a nonempty and bounded set $\mathcal{U} \subseteq {\mathbb{R}}^{p}$ such that ${\rho{({\mathcal{U} \circ {\mathbf{A}}})}} < 1$, then for any ${\rho{({\mathcal{U} \circ {\mathbf{A}}})}} < \rho < 1$, the system is $(\mathcal{U},\kappa,\rho)$-uniformly-stable with $\kappa = {{\phi{({\mathcal{U} \circ {\mathbf{A}}},\rho)}} \vee 1}$. We provide detailed discussion on this claim in Appendix [A](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#A1 "Appendix A Stability of Bilinear Dynamical Systems ‣ Finite Sample Identification of Partially Observed Bilinear Dynamical Systems"). Furthermore, we note that Definition (https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#Thmdefinition1 "Definition 1 ((𝒰,𝜅,𝜌)-uniform-stability). ‣ Uniform stability in bilinear dynamical systems: ‣ 2.2 Input Choice & Stability of Bilinear Dynamical Systems ‣ 2 Preliminaries ‣ Finite Sample Identification of Partially Observed Bilinear Dynamical Systems") naturally generalizes that introduced by \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib35)\]. Indeed, there the authors assume that there exists ${\mathbf{u}}^{\star}$ such that ${\rho{({{\mathbf{u}}^{\star} \circ {\mathbf{A}}})}} < 1$. This is equivalent to assuming that their system is $({\{{\mathbf{u}}^{\star}\}},\kappa,\rho)$-uniformly-stable for some $\kappa \geq 1$ and ${\rho{({{\mathbf{u}}^{\star} \circ {\mathbf{A}}})}} < \rho < 1$. As we shall make precise shortly, we need stronger requirements on the stability of the system in comparison with \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib35)\] because we are concerned with the task of identification. This requirement stems from the need to have persistence of excitation so that estimation is possible.

### Input choice

We consider that the inputs ${\{{\mathbf{u}}_{t}\}}_{t \geq 0}$ are sampled in an i.i.d. manner from some distribution $\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{u}}$ on ${\mathbb{R}}^{p}$. For ease of exposition, we will focus on the case where inputs are sampled uniformly at random from a sphere of radius $\sqrt{p}$, that is, ${\mathbf{u}}_{t} \sim {{Unif}{({\sqrt{p} \cdot \mathcal{S}^{p - 1}})}}$. More generally, as long as the inputs are isotropic and are bounded with high probability, our results will still hold at the expanse of longer proofs.

Under the condition that our estimation error is sufficiently small, each pair $(\mathbf{A}_{k},\mathbf{B})$ is controllable, each pair $(\mathbf{A}_{k},\mathbf{C})$ is observable, and $L \geq {2n}$, we can use classic Ho-Kalman algorithm \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib18)\] to estimate $\mathbf{A}_{0},\mathbf{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{A}_{p},\mathbf{B},\mathbf{C}$ up to a similarity transform, with robustness guarantees \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#bib.bib39)\]. Lastly, note that the estimate of $\mathbf{D}$ is obtained as the first $p$ columns of $\hat{\mathbf{G}}$.

### Persistence of Excitation

We show persistence of excitation which is necessary to ensure that the LSE is a consistent estimator. More precisely, we will establish that smallest singular value of the design matrix $\overset{\sim}{\mathbf{U}}$ whose rows correspond to ${\{{\overset{\sim}{\mathbf{u}}}_{t}^{\top}\}}_{t = L}^{T}$ is bounded from below by $\overset{\sim}{\Omega}{(\sqrt{{T - L} + 1})}$. One of the major sources of difficulty in establishing this persistence of excitation result challenging is the nonlinear dependence of ${\overset{\sim}{\mathbf{u}}}_{t}$ on ${\mathbf{u}}_{t - L},\ldots,{\mathbf{u}}_{t}$ for all $t \geq L$. We need to understand how distributional properties of the input impact the lower spectrum of ${\overset{\sim}{\mathbf{U}}}^{\top}\overset{\sim}{\mathbf{U}}$. To that end, we start by introducing the property of hypercontractivity.

### Definition 2 (Hypercontractivity)

A $p$-dimensional random vector $\mathbf{u}$ is $(4,2,\gamma)$-hypercontractive, if ${{\mathbb{E}}{\lbrack{({\mathbf{u}^{\top}\mathbf{x}})}^{4}\rbrack}} \leq {\gamma{\mathbb{E}}{\lbrack{({\mathbf{u}^{\top}\mathbf{x}})}^{2}\rbrack}^{2}}$, for all $\mathbf{x} \in {\mathbb{R}}^{p}$.

The $(4,2,\gamma)$-hypercontractivity property is satisfied by many classical distributions. Notably, a $p$-dimensional standard gaussian random vectors satisfies it with $\gamma = 3$, while $p$-dimensional random vectors sampled uniformly from $\sqrt{p} \cdot \mathcal{S}^{p - 1}$ satisfies $\gamma = {3/{({1 + {2/p}})}}$. We refer the reader to Appendix [B](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07652v2#A2 "Appendix B Proofs for Persistence of Excitation ‣ Finite Sample Identification of Partially Observed Bilinear Dynamical Systems") for a proof to these claims.

## Conclusion and Future Direction

We provide the first non-asymptotic learning bounds for partially observed BLDS. Given finite input-output data sampled from a single trajectory of BLDS, we learn its Markov-like parameters, provide an upper bound on the estimation error with $\overset{\sim}{\mathcal{O}}{({1/\sqrt{T}})}$ dependence, and provide a bound on the number of samples required, scaling as $\overset{\sim}{\Omega}\left( {({p + 1})}^{L + 1} \right)$. These parameters uniquely characterize the input-output map of a BLDS via nonlinear input features, hence, can be used to recover the state-space matrices. Our results hold under a novel notion of stability that generalizes the classic notion of stability for LDS to the BLDS.

There are several interesting future directions. First, can the exponential dependence on $L$ be avoided? We believe this can be done by carefully designing the inputs such that the number of Markov-like parameters do not grow exponentially in $L$. Second, can our results be extended to account for marginally-stable BLDS? This requires stabilization of a partially observed BLDS with unknown state-space matrices which itself is an interesting future direction. Other possible directions include exploring the benefit of regularization (e.g., Hankel nuclear norm regularization) for BLDS identification, exploring gradient-based methods for learning partially observed BLDS, and adaptive control of BLDS.
