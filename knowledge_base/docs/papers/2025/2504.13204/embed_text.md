## Introduction

Reconstructing 3D scenes from collections of 2D images is a fundamental challenge in computer vision \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib109 "Multiple view geometry in computer vision"), (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib107 "Dense reconstruction using 3d object shape priors"), (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib9 "Nerf: representing scenes as neural radiance fields for view synthesis")\], with applications in virtual and augmented reality \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib104 "Computer vision for autonomous vehicles: problems, datasets and state-of-the-art"), (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib103 "Overview of deep learning application on visual slam"), (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib23 "Relightable gaussian codec avatars"), (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib108 "Pointnet: deep learning on point sets for 3d classification and segmentation"), (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib24 "Drivable 3d gaussian avatars"), (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib40 "Sugar: surface-aligned gaussian splatting for efficient 3d mesh reconstruction and high-quality mesh rendering")\], robotics \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib105 "A survey of structure from motion"), (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib110 "Gs-slam: dense visual slam with 3d gaussian splatting"), (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib111 "Manigaussian: dynamic gaussian splatting for multi-task robotic manipulation")\], and content creation \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib106 "3D reconstruction methods for digital preservation of cultural heritage: a survey"), (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib114 "NeRF‐tex: neural reflectance field textures"), (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib113 "TeSTNeRF: text-driven 3d style transfer via cross-modal learning"), (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib112 "WaSt-3d: wasserstein-2 distance for scene-to-scene stylization on 3d gaussians")\]. The goal is to obtain high-quality 3D representations efficiently, enabling real-time rendering while maintaining reconstruction fidelity. However, achieving balance between efficiency, speed, and quality requires a representation that is both expressive and computationally efficient. NeRF-based models \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib9 "Nerf: representing scenes as neural radiance fields for view synthesis"), (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib68 "Plenoctrees for real-time rendering of neural radiance fields"), (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib94 "Mip-nerf 360: Unbounded anti-aliased neural radiance fields"), (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib43 "Instant neural graphics primitives with a multiresolution hash encoding"), (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib61 "Pixelnerf: neural radiance fields from one or few images"), (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib65 "Fastnerf: high-fidelity neural rendering at 200fps")\] control the trade-off between quality, computational cost, and representation capacity by designing network architectures and increasing the number of parameters. In contrast, point-based graphics \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib8 "Deep blending for free-viewpoint image-based rendering"), (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib115 "BakedSDF: Meshing Neural SDFs for Real-Time View Synthesis"), (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib108 "Pointnet: deep learning on point sets for 3d classification and segmentation")\] represent surfaces using discrete primitives, such as meshes or point clouds, offering more direct control over complexity but often struggling with quality and scalability.

Recently, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib10 "3D gaussian splatting for real-time radiance field rendering.")\] has emerged as a powerful and efficient alternative for representing 3D scenes. It models scenes as a set of optimized 3D Gaussians, mathematical primitives defined by their position, color, and spread. The method begins with sparse initialization, typically derived from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib89 "Structure-from-motion revisited")\], and progressively refines scene by adding splats to under-reconstructed regions. Through this densification process, 3DGS reaches high rendering quality while efficiently allocating computational resources.

However, this process is suboptimal. The original 3DGS detects under-reconstructed regions using the gradient norm of the photometric loss. But this metric often fails in high-frequency regions and does not align well with human perception. A separate branch of papers has proposed pixel-error-driven formulations \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib35 "Revising densification in gaussian splatting"), (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib90 "Pixel-gs: density control with pixel-aware gradient for 3d gaussian splatting"), (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib33 "Gaussianpro: 3d gaussian splatting with progressive propagation"), (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib76 "Taming 3dgs: high-quality radiance fields with limited resources")\], gradient calculation improvements \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib34 "AbsGS: recovering fine details in 3d gaussian splatting")\], and even treating 3DGS as Markov Chain Monte Carlo samples \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib96 "3D gaussian splatting as markov chain monte carlo")\]. Despite these efforts, accurately capturing fine details, particularly in high-frequency regions, remains a challenge, as illustrated in [Fig. 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#S0.F1 "In EDGS: Eliminating Densification for Efficient Convergence of 3DGS"). Furthermore, while each densification step is computationally efficient, the overall process is slow. It requires many update steps, as Gaussians must iteratively adjust their parameters before the model determines that additional splats are necessary. This results in a long optimization path, where individual Gaussians undergo multiple refinements before reaching their final states (see [Sec. 4.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#S4.SS5 "4.5 Ablation Studies ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ EDGS: Eliminating Densification for Efficient Convergence of 3DGS"), [Fig. 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#S3.F2 "In 3 Method ‣ EDGS: Eliminating Densification for Efficient Convergence of 3DGS") and [Fig. 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#S4.F4 "In Gaussian Motion and Convergence ‣ 4.5 Ablation Studies ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ EDGS: Eliminating Densification for Efficient Convergence of 3DGS")). Densification delays convergence, as it takes many iterations for the model to identify areas requiring higher reconstruction fidelity. These challenges raise an important question: can we bypass densification entirely?

In this paper, we propose a direct initialization strategy that eliminates the need for incremental densification used in the original 3DGS method. Rather than waiting for the model to gradually fill in missing details, we precompute a dense set of 3D Gaussians by triangulating dense 2D correspondences across multiple input views. Knowing the viewing rays for each correspondence pixel and the camera poses, we recover 3D positions of Gaussians by triangulating matched pixels between image pairs. This allows us to assign each Gaussian well-informed initial parameters, such as position, color, and scale, from the start. To summarize, we replace the slow iterative densification process of the scene with a dense initialization. As a result, each Gaussian is immediately supervised by rich per-pixel photometric signal, allowing for efficient optimization of the entire scene.

Although this initialization is noisy (see [Fig. 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#S0.F1 "In EDGS: Eliminating Densification for Efficient Convergence of 3DGS")), we show that it remains robust and leads to faster convergence. Our experiments, quantitatively and qualitatively, confirm that EDGS yields higher reconstruction quality, shorter training time, fewer Gaussians, and eliminates the need for densification. Our contributions can be summarized as follows:

We introduce a novel dense initialization for 3D Gaussian Splatting, based on the sampling distribution of triangulated multi-view correspondences, which effectively replaces the traditional incremental refinement process.

EDGS achieves faster convergence and higher reconstruction quality than prior 3DGS methods. We further analyze how the proposed initialization affects the optimization trajectories of individual Gaussians.

Our initialization improves reconstruction without modifying the optimization algorithm, making it compatible with other 3DGS methods. This makes it a complementary component that can be seamlessly integrated with adaptive densification strategies to further enhance performance.

### Densification

Several studies suggest that using an effective strategy for splat densification can significantly enhance performance. RevDev \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib35 "Revising densification in gaussian splatting")\] introduced a per-pixel error function as a criterion for densification. AbsGS \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib34 "AbsGS: recovering fine details in 3d gaussian splatting")\] addressed the issue of gradient collision during the detection of under-reconstructed regions. MiniSplatting \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib97 "Mini-splatting: representing scenes with a constrained number of gaussians")\] proposed a densification approach that incorporates both screen-space and world-space information. ScaffoldGS \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib30 "Scaffold-gs: structured 3d gaussians for view-adaptive rendering")\] introduced anchor points and a growth algorithm to optimize Gaussians distribution. Meanwhile, 3DGS-MCMC \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib96 "3D gaussian splatting as markov chain monte carlo")\] reformulated densification as a Markov Chain Monte Carlo sampling process, enabling an efficient Gaussian distribution across scene. In contrast, we propose an improved initialization method that avoids densification altogether, eliminating the need to detect under-reconstructed regions.

### Efficiency

A number of recent works aim to enhance the efficiency of 3DGS. One approach leverages pre-trained neural networks as priors to guide reconstruction \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib77 "Mvsplat: efficient 3d gaussian splatting from sparse multi-view images"), (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib79 "Triplane meets gaussian splatting: fast and generalizable single-view 3d reconstruction with transformers"), (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib80 "Instantsplat: unbounded sparse-view pose-free gaussian splatting in 40 seconds"), (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib83 "Depthsplat: connecting gaussian splatting and depth")\]. This data-driven strategy enables rapid reconstruction with high quality, particularly in sparse-view scenarios. We focus on dense-view reconstruction. Another area of research targets the optimization of 3DGS by refining the differentiable rasterizer \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib81 "DISTWAR: fast differentiable rendering on raster-based rendering pipelines"), (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib76 "Taming 3dgs: high-quality radiance fields with limited resources"), (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib82 "Flashgs: efficient 3d gaussian splatting for large-scale and high-resolution rendering")\] or improving the framework itself \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib88 "Gsplat: an open-source library for Gaussian splatting")\]. Separately, 3DGS-LM \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib85 "3DGS-lm: faster gaussian-splatting optimization with levenberg-marquardt")\] proposes a Levenberg-Marquardt optimizer that integrates with the 3DGS rasterizer and can be adapted to other rasterization methods. Our approach instead focuses on improving the initialization process, which is compatible with these optimizations.

### Initialization

Recent works, such as RAIN-GS \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib84 "Relaxing accurate initialization constraint for 3d gaussian splatting")\] and 3DGS-MCMC \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib96 "3D gaussian splatting as markov chain monte carlo")\], shows that random initialization can match the performance of 3DGS. In contrast, RadSplat \[(https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#bib.bib95 "RadSplat: radiance field-informed gaussian splatting for robust real-time rendering with 900+ fps")\] initializes from points extracted using pretrained NeRFs to improve quality, though it requires 9 hours of training. EDGS departs from both approaches by emphasizing efficiency while outperforming quality-focused methods.

## Method

Figure 2: EDGS initializes Gaussians closer to their final positions, resulting in shorter optimization trajectories and faster convergence to high-quality reconstructions.

Our goal is to initialize a dense set of Gaussian splats ([Sec. 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#S3.SS1 "3.1 Preliminaries ‣ 3 Method ‣ EDGS: Eliminating Densification for Efficient Convergence of 3DGS")). Instead of incrementally adding information via photometric loss, we leverage all available 2D image information from the start ([Sec. 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#S3.SS2 "3.2 Extract information from 2D prior ‣ 3 Method ‣ EDGS: Eliminating Densification for Efficient Convergence of 3DGS")). We first triangulate dense pixel correspondences into 3D space ([Sec. 3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#S3.SS3 "3.3 Splats triangulation ‣ 3 Method ‣ EDGS: Eliminating Densification for Efficient Convergence of 3DGS")). Then, we aggregate semantic confidence and geometric consistency across neighboring views to build a sampling distribution $\mathbf{p}^{i}$ for each reference image ([Sec. 3.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#S3.SS4 "3.4 Sampling distribution ‣ 3 Method ‣ EDGS: Eliminating Densification for Efficient Convergence of 3DGS")), from which splats are sampled. Finally, we initialize spherical harmonics for the sampled splats ([Sec. 3.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#S3.SS5 "3.5 Spherical harmonics ‣ 3 Method ‣ EDGS: Eliminating Densification for Efficient Convergence of 3DGS")).

### Sampling distribution

As defined in [Eq. 11](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#S3.E11 "In 3.4 Sampling distribution ‣ 3 Method ‣ EDGS: Eliminating Densification for Efficient Convergence of 3DGS"), the sampling distribution $\mathbf{p}^{i}$ combines two terms: the correspondence-based distribution $\mathbf{p}^{ij}\text{corr}$, which reflects matcher confidence, and the geometry-based distribution $\mathbf{p}_{\text{proj}}^{ij}$, which penalizes re-projection errors. These components complement each other: confidence alone captures semantic reliability but cannot enforce geometric consistency, whereas the re-projection term removes mismatched or unstable correspondences. To maintain spatial coverage, we perform uniform sampling over confidence-thresholded matches $\mathbf{c}^{ij}$, preventing bias toward high-confidence but spatially clustered regions. The combination of both terms yields a balanced, geometry-aware initialization that consistently outperforms using either component in isolation ([Tab. 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#S4.T6 "In Gaussian Motion and Convergence ‣ 4.5 Ablation Studies ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ EDGS: Eliminating Densification for Efficient Convergence of 3DGS")).

Figure 5: Dense correspondences for a pair of images from bicycle scene. The top row shows matched keypoints, and the bottom row visualizes the confidence of correspondences in the neighboring image. The matching model ℳ is RoMa. Correspondence confidence is not uniform across the scene.

To assess the importance of the re-projection term, we replace the right-hand side of [Eq. 11](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#S3.E11 "In 3.4 Sampling distribution ‣ 3 Method ‣ EDGS: Eliminating Densification for Efficient Convergence of 3DGS") with ${\max_{j \in {{\mathbb{I}}i}}\mathbf{p}_{\text{corr}}^{ij}}{(k)}$. Conversely, removing $\mathbf{p}_{\text{corr}}^{ij}$ entirely is infeasible as we still require match locations, but we can sample points proportionally to confidence by setting $\mathbf{p}_{\text{corr}}^{ij} \propto \mathbf{c}^{ij}$. As shown in [Tab. 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#S4.T6 "In Gaussian Motion and Convergence ‣ 4.5 Ablation Studies ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ EDGS: Eliminating Densification for Efficient Convergence of 3DGS"), both modifications degrade performance, with the confidence-only variant having the strongest negative effect. This is expected: sampling in proportion to $\mathbf{c}^{ij}$ breaks uniform spatial coverage and concentrates splats around high-confidence edges or boundaries, leading to poorer initialization. Disabling both geometric and confidence cues further reduces accuracy and produces visibly more floaters. In [Fig. 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#S4.F5 "In Sampling distribution ‣ 4.5 Ablation Studies ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ EDGS: Eliminating Densification for Efficient Convergence of 3DGS"), we visualize a set of keypoints extracted from a single pair of images. The results highlight that we need to sample keypoints from the image more uniformly, rather than focusing solely on keypoints with high confidence.

Figure 6: PSNR and LPIPS curves show saturation as we independently increase any of three parameters: number of reference views, number of nearest-neighbor, and number of sampled correspondences per reference view. 3DGS is provided for reference.

### Robustness to noise

EDGS exhibits strong resilience to imperfections in the initial correspondences, which may arise from triangulation inaccuracies or suboptimal matches produced by $\mathcal{M}$. To quantitatively assess this robustness, we perturb the initialized Gaussian parameters with additive Gaussian noise $\epsilon \sim {\mathcal{N}{(0,\sigma)}}$ applied independently to either spatial coordinates or color values. We then evaluate reconstruction quality across varying noise levels $\sigma$. Formally, noise $\epsilon$ is injected into the color parameters ${\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{c}$ and coordinate parameters ${\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{x}$ of the initialized Gaussians. This design isolates the influence of each parameter type on convergence.

As shown in [Fig. 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#S4.F7 "In Spherical harmonics ‣ 4.5 Ablation Studies ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ EDGS: Eliminating Densification for Efficient Convergence of 3DGS"), increasing coordinate or color noise leads to gradual degradation in PSNR and LPIPS. Remarkably, EDGS maintains substantially higher robustness to color perturbations than to spatial ones, supporting our claim that the method's primary advantage lies in reducing unnecessary Gaussian movement during optimization. Even under moderate coordinate noise, overall reconstruction quality remains stable, demonstrating the inherent regularization of our initialization. Notably, our method remains stable even with small amounts of added noise, likely because the initialization itself is already inherently noisy, as shown in [Fig. A3](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#A8.F3 "In Appendix H Notation ‣ EDGS: Eliminating Densification for Efficient Convergence of 3DGS"). All experiments are conducted on the Mip-NeRF360 dataset. In [Fig. A2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13204v2#A6.F2 "In Robustness to noise in initialization ‣ Appendix F Additional visual results ‣ EDGS: Eliminating Densification for Efficient Convergence of 3DGS"), we visualized initialization for scene garden, which was noised with different scales for both coordinates (first row) and colors (second row).

## Conclusion

We introduce a new initialization strategy for 3D Gaussian Splatting that removes the need for iterative densification. The approach relies on carefully sampling 2D correspondences that are both geometrically consistent and provide uniform, dense coverage of the scene.

Our method reaches state-of-the-art performance without any densification and matches efficiency-oriented methods with substantially fewer optimization steps. Moreover, EDGS functions as a plug-and-play initialization for adaptive density control techniques, improving reconstruction quality without increasing training time or Gaussian count, making it a practical and broadly applicable enhancement for 3D reconstruction pipelines.
